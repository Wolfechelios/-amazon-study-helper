# STAR Interview Prep

STAR means Situation, Task, Action, Result.

Use it like this:

- Situation: Set the scene in one sentence.
- Task: Say what you were responsible for.
- Action: Say what you personally did.
- Result: Give the outcome, lesson, metric, or prevention step.

## Data center technical answer pattern

Situation: A system, rack, ticket, or process had an issue that could affect uptime, safety, or quality.
Task: I had to identify the issue, protect service, follow procedure, and communicate clearly.
Action: I checked facts first, used the right tool or process, documented what I found, and escalated with evidence if needed.
Result: The issue was isolated, the next step was clear, and the work was safer, cleaner, or faster next time.

## Example: CPU usage

Situation: A host was reported as slow.
Task: I needed to determine whether CPU load was the cause.
Action: I checked top or htop, load average, process usage, run queue, and logs to identify the source.
Result: I could explain whether it was normal workload, a runaway process, or something that needed escalation.

## Example: network link issue

Situation: A server link was down or unstable.
Task: I needed to isolate whether the issue was host side, cable, optic, or switch side.
Action: I started at Layer 1, checked seating, link light, cable path, optic data, interface counters, and far-end status.
Result: I narrowed the problem and gave the next team a clear, evidence-based handoff.

## Example: safety

Situation: I noticed a task condition that was unclear or unsafe.
Task: I had to protect people, equipment, and the work process.
Action: I paused, communicated the concern, verified the correct method, and continued only when the risk was controlled.
Result: The work was completed safely and without avoidable rework.

## Example: ticket documentation

Situation: I worked a ticket that another technician or team might inherit.
Task: I needed the ticket to be useful after my shift.
Action: I documented the symptom, checks performed, timestamps, serials or location, action taken, validation, and next step.
Result: The handoff was clear and the next person did not have to start from zero.

## Example: ownership

Situation: I saw a technical issue that affected the job beyond my immediate task.
Task: I needed to make sure the outcome was handled, not just my portion.
Action: I took responsibility, communicated status, coordinated with the right people, documented the work, and followed through.
Result: The issue was closed cleanly and did not get passed around.
